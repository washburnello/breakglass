Daily Plan Guide, Grades 9–12

How to use this guide

High school is independent study with adult oversight. The student owns the schedule, works from this page and the tracker, and brings finished work to a fixed weekly review; the parent checks products, not seat time. Credits form naturally when the year's outcomes below are met. Everything runs offline from printed or bundled texts.

Daily rhythm — about 4½ hours, reading included

Independent blocks in a fixed order; deep work goes first

A transcript-worthy pace needs roughly five days of this per week. If a day collapses, cut composition and history before mathematics and reading.

The year at a glance

Outcomes that mark a completed year. The student should be able to demonstrate each without notes.

Literature & Rhetoric

  • Reads demanding older prose — epic, drama, essay, speech — and tracks its argument, not just plot
  • Analyzes an author's appeals, structure, tone, and intended audience in a short analytic essay
  • Contributes to a discussion with citations from the text rather than impressions
  • Traces a primary source's bias: who wrote it, for whom, what it leaves out

Composition

  • Writes a sourced essay of five paragraphs or more with real citations in one consistent format
  • Runs a multiweek pipeline: research question, outline, draft, revision, final proof
  • Shifts register on demand: lab report, persuasive essay, business letter, application statement
  • Cuts a draft by ten percent without losing meaning

Mathematics (Alg→Geo→Adv)

  • Advances through Algebra 1 → Geometry → advanced Algebra at mastery pace, not calendar pace
  • Solves linear systems and quadratics; manipulates radicals and rational expressions cleanly
  • Writes geometric proofs citing the postulates and theorems used
  • Models real situations — loans, mixtures, motion — with functions and checks units

Laboratory Sciences

  • Designs a household-lab investigation: hypothesis, procedure, control, data table, error notes
  • Reports in standard format — aim, method, data, interpretation — from a bound lab notebook
  • Reads labels like a scientist: concentrations, hazards, incompatibilities
  • Explains core physical and life-science laws from evidence gathered, not authority quoted

History & Civics

  • Narrates cause-and-effect chains across eras instead of isolated dates
  • Summarizes founding documents and landmark speeches with their original context intact
  • Corroborates any contested claim against a second independent account
  • Explains how lawmaking and the levels of government interact, using a current example

Economics & Life Skills

  • Keeps a written personal budget; explains interest and compound growth working for and against you
  • Compares cost per unit before buying and plans a meal, repair, or project to a price
  • Demonstrates durable skills: cooking, mending, first-aid refreshers, a working résumé
  • Reads a sample contract or lease and lists its obligations before "signing"

Bible & Apologetics

  • Studies passages with observe–interpret–apply, cross-checked between translations
  • States another author's argument in its strongest form before answering it
  • Gives reasons and evidence for core beliefs while representing opposing views honestly
  • Separates essentials from preferences in discussion; disagrees without contempt

Materials — austere household edition

  • Printed open-licensed textbooks for math and science; public-domain rhetoric and grammar texts
  • Bundled classics and documents from the Book Library, printed or on screen
  • Bound lab notebook, composition notebooks, graph paper, scientific calculator (solar if possible)
  • Household lab stock: jars, funnel, measuring cups, thermometer, baking soda, vinegar, salt, sugar, oil, yeast; garden soil and seeds; safety glasses if obtainable
  • Sample forms for life skills: a practice lease, a job application, blank budget sheets
  • A printed dictionary and a style sheet; citations checked against books, not memory

Laboratory discipline

Kitchen chemistry is real chemistry: goggles on for reactive demos, no sealed containers over heat, and never taste results. Never combine bleach with vinegar or ammonia — toxic gas follows. Heat work obeys Heating & Cooking Safely; water purification labs obey Water Treatment; soil and garden work ends at the sink per Sanitation & Hygiene.

Assessment without tests

  • Written narrations: after readings, summarize from memory in a paragraph — the fastest honest measure of comprehension.
  • Self-checked problem sets: work against an answer key; every miss goes into the error log with the reason it happened, then gets redone cold.
  • Portfolio review: essays, lab reports, and projects collect dated in one folder; the monthly review is presented by the student.

Bible & apologetics block

A passage-study method: observe, interpret, apply

  1. Observe. Two slow readings. List actors, structure, repeated terms, tensions. Record only what the text says — interpretation waits its turn.
  2. Interpret. Establish what it meant to the first hearers: compare translations, follow cross-references, note genre, then compress the main point to one sentence.
  3. Apply. One concrete change in belief or behavior for the week, written down. Honest application is downstream of honest observation.

An apologetics reading approach

  • Understand before judging — restate the author's case in its strongest form first
  • Sort each text into claims made, evidence offered, and assumptions carried
  • Trace sources: check at least one citation yourself before accepting it
  • Write the best counterargument you can, then answer it in writing
  • Discuss to understand: represent opponents honestly, concede true points, keep tone warm

Rhythm: one substantial chapter or article per week with a one-page response, and one discussion night a month where a hard question gets aired fairly from both sides.
