Schoolhouse

A complete school in a folder

The daily plans below are thin but complete — enough to run a real schoolyear today, deepening over time. Everything runs with zero power and zero screens: print a plan, tape it to the wall, and teach with paper and pencils. Progress tracking lives in the Progress Tracker, not on these pages.

How the Schoolhouse teaches

Short lessons, real books, honest practice, and recitation out loud. That is the whole method, and it is the oldest one there is. Lessons stay brief because attention is a muscle, not a warehouse; books are real because children rise to good language; practice is daily because skill grows by repetition; recitation is out loud because saying a thing proves you have it.

It is a one-room school: many ages at one table. Every band studies the same subjects on the same morning — the little ones for minutes, the big ones for hours, each at their own depth. One parent or teacher can run all of it.

How this section grows

  • Start with the daily plan for your band. Print it — it is designed to be taped to a wall and followed from paper.
  • Open-education packs (open textbooks, problem sets, and simulations) arrive over time as optional add-ons; the plans never require them.
  • In hosted mode, a video classroom joins later for families that want it. It is a bonus, never a dependency.
  • Curriculum here is original and faith-integrated in the plainest sense: an ordinary school day with a daily Bible story or passage, a memory verse, and a character conversation. No denomination, no franchise.
