Pet Emergency Prep

The evacuate-together rule

If it isn't safe for you, it isn't safe for them. Plan routes and destinations that accept animals before you need them — many public shelters only take service animals, so identify pet-friendly hotels, family, boarding kennels, and vets along two escape directions.

The pet go-kit

One container per animal, grab-ready by the door. Store where the human go-bags live so nobody forgets them.

Identification layers

  1. Collar tag with a current phone number — the fastest reunification path there is.
  2. Microchip — collars slip; chips don't. Verify registration points to a phone you'll actually answer during an emergency.
  3. Current photos — face and full-body, stored offline in your phone and printed in the kit.

If you must shelter in place

  • Bring outdoor animals inside early — frightened animals bolt long before the weather arrives.
  • Confine nervous animals to one quiet interior room with their kit; scared cats hide under furniture you can't reach later.
  • Keep leashes handy even indoors: post-disaster doors and fences fail.

The neighbor handshake

Trade keys and kit locations with one trusted neighbor: "If I can't get home, feed and water my animals, they're in the garage." Write the arrangement down — both sides. This five-minute conversation rescues more pets than any gadget.

Livestock note

Large animals need 24–72 hours' notice to relocate, so their triggers fire earlier: haul arrangements made in advance, trailers checked monthly, and a written "release or relocate" decision point for fires/floods. Never release animals onto roads.
