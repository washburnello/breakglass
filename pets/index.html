Pets & Animals

They count on you twice over

Animals cannot store their own supplies or read an evacuation notice. Everything they need in an outage must already be on your shelf — and the single most common disaster mistake with pets is assuming "we'll figure it out when it happens." This section now goes beyond emergencies: raising and training guides for chickens, dogs, cats, and horses live here too.

Quick numbers

  • 72 hours minimum of pet food and water stored alongside the human supply — a week is better.
  • If you evacuate, they evacuate. Leaving animals behind, even "just for a night," is how pets are lost.
  • Identification beats luck: collar tag + microchip + a current photo on your phone.
  • Raising and training guides above scale the same principles up: chickens start with a brooder, horses with an honest budget.

Care guidance here is general; your veterinarian knows your animal. Grounded in public-domain FEMA/ready.gov pet preparedness guidance.
