Water Treatment

Assume nothing is safe until treated

Clear mountain water can still carry organisms that lay you flat for days. When the safety of a water source is unknown, treat every drop before drinking, brushing teeth, or washing dishes that touch mouths.

Clarify first, then treat

Treatment works poorly in cloudy water. If water is murky:

  1. Let it stand so sediment settles (30–60 minutes helps a lot).
  2. Pour off the clearer top layer, or filter it through cloth, a coffee filter, or paper towel.
  3. Treat the clarified water using any method below.

Method 1 — Boiling (most reliable)

Rolling boil = bubbles you cannot stir away

No thermometer needed. Boiling kills everything that matters. Flat taste comes from lost air — shake the cooled water in a sealed jar or pour between two containers to restore it. Fuel-hungry, but unbeatable when fuel exists.

Method 2 — Unscented household bleach

Regular unscented liquid bleach only. Check the concentration percentage on the label:

  1. Add drops, stir, wait 30 minutes.
  2. Smell test: a slight chlorine smell means success. No smell? Repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes.

Bleach has limits

Check your bottle: plain sodium hypochlorite 5–9%, unscented, not expired. Splash-less, thickened, scented, or "color-safe" formulas are not for water treatment. Bleach weakens with age — a bottle open over a year disinfects less reliably than its label claims.

Method 3 — Filters

  • Good backpacking-style filters remove protozoa and bacteria — the most common wilderness troublemakers.
  • Most squeeze/straw filters do not stop viruses, which matter mainly where human sewage contaminates water. After floodwater contact, boil or chemically treat even filtered water.
  • Ceramic and charcoal countertop systems vary wildly by model; trust them only as far as their tested rating.

Sources ranked best to worst

  1. Rainwater caught in clean containers
  2. Moving water upstream of people and animals
  3. Lakes and ponds (surface scum — take water below it)
  4. Wells and taps of unknown status
  5. Floodwater, stagnant pools, water near latrines or industry — absolute last resort, treat twice

Do not drink at all

Any water with a fuel or chemical smell, foam or discoloration near industrial areas, or dead animals floating nearby. Chemical contamination does not boil out, filter out, or bleach away. Find another source.

After treating: keep it clean

Store treated water in scrubbed containers with tight lids, labeled with the date. Use within six months, rotating fresher stock forward — see Water Storage for container prep and quantities.
