Dosing by Weight

Educational only — verify before every dose

These tables reflect the standard amounts printed on over-the-counter packaging for two common fever and pain medicines. They are for reference when original packaging is lost. Every dose must be verified against the actual product label or a pharmacist/doctor — formulations and concentrations vary by brand and country. When in doubt, dose low or ask.

The five rules

  1. Dose by weight, never by age, whenever you know the weight.
  2. Use only one medicine containing each ingredient. Many cold remedies secretly contain acetaminophen — double dosing is the most common overdose there is.
  3. Use the measuring device meant for it: the cup or syringe from the box, or a kitchen syringe — never kitchen spoons, which vary by 2–3×.
  4. Write down time and amount of every dose when several people share caregiving.
  5. No aspirin under 19 during viral illness — risk of Reye's syndrome.

Which one, when?

Either handles fever and pain. Ibuprofen also calms swelling (sprains, sunburn) but needs kidneys and hydration working well — with vomiting, diarrhea, or dehydration, acetaminophen is the safer pick. Do not alternate them unless directed professionally; alternation is where record-keeping errors become overdoses.

Adults without scales

Standard adult doses printed on packaging apply to anyone roughly 96 lb (44 kg) or more: acetaminophen 325–650 mg per dose, ibuprofen 200–400 mg per dose, taken with the frequency limits above.
