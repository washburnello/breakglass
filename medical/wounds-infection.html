Wound Care & Infection

The whole skill in one line

Clean the wound well once, keep it covered and moist-but-not-soaked, then watch it daily. Most wounds heal on their own when cleaned properly at the start.

At a glance

  • Flush with clean running water, let it run through — do not scrub raw tissue.
  • Change dressings daily, or whenever soaked through.
  • Bites, deep punctures, dirty wounds: do not close — high infection risk.
  • Red streaks, fever, spreading redness = professional care fast.
  • Dirty or deep wound within five years of your last tetanus booster warrants a new one when care is reachable.

Cleaning, step by step

  1. Wash your own hands first — soap and water, or sanitizer if that is all there is.
  2. Stop bleeding with direct pressure (see Severe Bleeding for heavy flow).
  3. Flush the wound itself with clean running water — boiled-and-cooled or treated if your water is suspect. Let it run through, do not scrub the raw tissue.
  4. Wash around the wound with soap and a cloth; keep soap out of the open cut, it stings and irritates.
  5. Lift out debris you can see with tweezers cleaned in alcohol or flame. Anything deeply embedded stays put — pack around it and get help.
  6. Pat dry around it, apply thin antibiotic ointment, cover with a clean dressing.

To close or not to close

When closure strips help — and when they trap trouble
SituationClosure strips?
Edges fall together cleanly, wound is fresh (< 8 hours), not gapingYes — pull edges snug, strip across perpendicular, keep dry 5 days
Gapes open, ragged, or has visible dirt/debris insideNo — pack lightly with clean gauze and let it heal from the bottom up
Animal or human bite, deep puncture, rusty/dirty objectNo closure — high infection risk; professional evaluation matters here

Daily life rule: reopen a closed wound only if infection signs appear under it — trapped pus is worse than a slow-healing gap.

Dressing changes

  • Change once a day, or whenever it soaks through.
  • If it sticks, wet it with clean saline or boiled-cooled water instead of tearing.
  • Re-clean gently, re-ointment, re-cover each change.
  • Keep it dry in the shower: plastic bag taped over it beats a soggy dressing.

Infection: catch it early

Check every day at the dressing change. Normal healing shows mild pinkness right at the edge, fading day by day. Infection marches outward:

  • Redness spreading beyond the wound edge or a red streak tracking toward the heart
  • Increasing pain, warmth, and swelling after day two instead of decreasing
  • Cloudy yellow-green pus, or a bad smell
  • Fever or swollen glands near the wound

Escalate now for

Red streaks running from the wound · fever with spreading redness · rapidly increasing pain · pus under a closed wound · any infected bite · diabetes or immune problems plus any infection sign. These need professional care fast, not more home treatment.

Abscesses and splinters

A pocket of pus wants to drain, but squeezing pushes infection inward. Warm, moist compresses several times a day let small abscesses open on their own. Deep or painful ones belong with a professional. Tetanus boosters last about ten years — a dirty or deep wound inside five years since your booster also warrants a new one when care is reachable.
