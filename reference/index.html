Educational only — verify against packaging and professional advice

These are dense, at-hand numbers for moments when the original label, manual, or almanac is gone. Critical values — especially medicine doses — must be checked against actual packaging or a professional. The medication tables mirror Medical: Dosing by Weight, which links here.

At a glance

  • Bleach (unscented, clear water): 8 drops per gallon at 6% · 6 drops per gallon at 8.25%
  • Boiling water: 1 minute below 6,500 ft · 3 minutes above
  • No-power food clock: fridge 4 hours · full freezer 48 h · half-full freezer 24 h
  • Stored water: 1 gallon per person per day

Water treatment

Unscented liquid bleach in clear water — stir, wait 30 minutes; a slight chlorine smell means success. Double the drops if cloudy (after settling/filtering).

Purification tablets: dosing and contact time vary by brand. Most chlorine-dioxide tablets treat 1 liter in about 30 minutes; Cryptosporidium can demand up to 4 hours. The printed directions are the authority — expired tablets lose strength.

Convention, not law: Channel 1 is the informal nationwide calling/travel channel — listen first, keep calls brief, then move to a working channel. Channel 3 is a widely mentioned prepper/community emergency-liaison habit; no channel carries any official emergency assignment.
License: FRS needs none. GMRS requires an FCC license — about $35, 10 years, no test, one license covers your immediate family.

SAME/EAS: weather radios with SAME (Specific Area Message Encoding) can alarm only for chosen counties — look up your county’s 6-digit SAME code from the NWS before you need it and write it inside the radio’s battery door. Emergency Alert System messages ride this same network to broadcast stations.
